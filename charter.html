The charter

What this station is, and the rules it keeps.

What it is

A free public radio station carrying recordings of real imaginal encounters. Somebody is guided into a place that does not exist. They describe what they find. The tape runs, and the best of it goes out on an appointed hour.

Nothing here is acted. That is the entire premise, and it is the only thing that cannot be traded away.

The rules

  1. The recording is the material. Not a show about the practice. If a broadcast could have been written in advance, it does not belong on this station.
  2. Consent runs backwards. The traveler hears the cut and says yes before it airs. Never the other way round. Authorising the transmission is part of coming back.
  3. Every broadcast asks one question and then leaves real silence. Long enough that an answer forms whether or not it is spoken. That silence is the listener's part.
  4. The advertisements are part of the story. They always were.
  5. Thirteen and then boxed. A program that never ends is a feed. Each program runs its length, becomes an object, and the next one takes a different form.
  6. An appointed hour. Sundays at ten, Mountain. Repetition at a set time is the difference between a station and a library.
  7. Nothing gets fixed in the edit. Hesitation, silence, a traveller losing the thread and finding it again: that is the evidence the thing was real.

Why radio

Radio has no picture. It arrives and you supply the image, which puts it in exactly the register this practice cares about: more tangible than a thought, less fixed than a thing. Every listener's version differs and none of them is wrong.

AM in particular drifts. It fades and returns, it carries noise, and at night it bounces off the ionosphere and reaches you from further away than it has any business reaching. The medium behaves like the subject.

Why a real signal

1690 kHz, unlicensed and legal at very low power, covering about one block. Almost nobody will ever hear it that way, and that is fine. It matters that there is an actual carrier in actual air. Otherwise this is a podcast wearing a costume.

Who it is for

People who join us, people who write in, people who we imagined. Three kinds of participant, and the third one is not a joke.

Sleepy Planet AM is made by Mike Marrotte. Broadcasts from somewhere, sent to nowhere, picked up from anywhere.
